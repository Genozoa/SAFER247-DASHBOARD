SAFER 247 · RESPONDER ANDROID APP · IA + SCREEN STRUCTURE REVIEW

Three competing navigation structures, each built against one compact 360×640 screen.

Below: my reading of the context doc, the assumptions I need you to confirm or correct, and three full IA directions with three screens each. Pick a direction (or mix parts) and I'll build out the remaining screens — search/filter, call sign, beaconing detail — inside it.

WHAT THE SETTING DICTATES
Gloves, rain, noon sun, night, battery scarcity, 360×640 dp. So: words instead of icons (an icon under stress is a guess), 56–64 dp minimum targets, one primary action per screen, metadata in monospace so call signs and coordinates are read as data — never a dense card. Light ground is the default because sunlight legibility beats night legibility in frequency; 1c shows the night treatment.
THE STATUS PROBLEM
Four system states compete for space: radio link, background listening, beaconing, and per-message transmit status. My rule — the first three collapse into one line of system truth directly under the app bar, in the same place on every screen; per-message status stays on the message, never duplicated in a banner. Beaconing is the only state allowed to shout, because it is the only one that costs you incoming messages.
WHAT I DE-EMPHASIZED, ON PURPOSE
Search and filter are not on the feed — they live behind one word in the app bar. Scanning the last ten messages is the real field task; searching is a base-camp task. Coordinates are one quiet mono line, not a hero, since with no map they are only read aloud over voice. Call sign editing is buried one level into Setup — it is set once, and a mis-tap there corrupts your identity on every future broadcast.
ASSUMPTIONS — PLEASE CONFIRM OR CORRECT
A1  The responder reads far more than they send. Receiving is the app's resting state, so every direction opens on traffic or one tap from it.
A2  A quick message is usually 3–10 words, typed one-handed. The composer is a single field, not a form.
A3  Four incident types is the whole list, so type selection can be four big tiles rather than a dropdown.
A4  An image at 1–3 minutes of channel time is a deliberate, rare decision — so attaching one always shows its cost in time, and never auto-sends.
A5  Retransmit is normal behaviour, not error recovery. It sits on every sent message at equal weight, and needs no explanation copy.
A6  The phone lives in a pocket or mount with the screen off. Notifications, not the feed, are how a new message is actually noticed.

One stream, two densities, two send buttons

The channel is one thing, so the feed is one screen and one scroll — that is 1a. Within it, chatter compresses to a single line and reports open into cards: the separation the requirements ask for is carried by density and form, not by a second destination. REPORTS is a filter of that same scroll, so nothing you can see in it is hidden from ALL.

Compose stays as 1b had it: a docked bar with QUICK MESSAGE at full width and REPORT beside it. No floating pill, no intermediate "what kind of message?" step, both entries permanently visible and thumb-reachable.

STATUS, IN EXACTLY THREE PLACES
One 30 dp strip under the feed's tabs carries link method, listening and beacon state — nowhere else, on no other screen, in the same word order every time. Per-message transmit status appears only on your own messages and only when abnormal. The lock-screen row is the third and last place. No banners, no toasts, no duplicate badges.
WHAT I DEMOTED, AND THE COST
Coordinates off the chatter rows — a quick message's position is in detail only; if responders routinely read positions off the feed, this is wrong and the row needs a third line. Search behind FIND — costs a tap, buys 56 dp of feed. No corrupt-decode inbox — a failed decode appears as a quiet row in the feed rather than a screen of its own, which the reliability requirement may want more loudly.
THE HONEST WEAKNESS
Merging 1a's tab row with 1b's docked buttons puts 294 dp of permanent chrome on a 640 dp screen — 46%. Five messages still fit, but this is the ceiling; nothing else can be added to the feed frame. If it needs to breathe more, the two candidates are collapsing the tab row on scroll, or moving BEACON out of the bottom nav into the status strip, which would drop the nav to two destinations.
NOT YET DESIGNED
Call sign edit screen, full-screen image viewer, filtered-results state of the feed, first-run setup, and the radio-disconnected variant of the status strip. Say the word and they go in this same frame. Distress from 1c is deliberately absent here pending MDRRMO protocol.

Reports are a different kind of object

A report is a structured, actionable record; chatter is ephemeral. So they get different screens, different densities, and different reading postures. Chatter compresses to one line per message — you skim it. Reports open up — you act on them. The cost is a fourth destination.

PRIMARY ACTION
Traffic: skim and send a quick message. Reports: read the newest report, or file one. Each screen keeps its own primary button docked at the bottom, so the thumb never travels.

1cAction-first home, night treatment, with the distress affordance

The app opens on what you came to do

This one rejects the feed-first premise. If the phone comes out of a pocket under stress, it is usually to send something — the radio already told you what is happening. So home is two enormous buttons and a count of what you missed; traffic is one tap away. It is also the only direction with a distress send, rendered dark for night work.
